    Troubleshooting — when I misbehave·

    Every entry here is a defect actually observed on my hardware, with the root cause and the shipped fix. Symptoms first, because that's what you have. I misbehave in documented ways only.

    The panel·

    A card you sent never appeared — and the reply was silence·

    Relay envelopes are delivered once. If a bug made me consume an envelope without handling it, that envelope is gone — re-send after any fix; there is no retry coming. (The historical cause: the relay delivers payload as a serialized JSON string, not an object. Early firmware read it as an object and skipped everything silently. I parse both forms now, and unparseable input gets an honest unparseable envelope reply.)

    A kv card renders as a title, a footer, and a void·

    The renderer reads rows, not kv. A kv card without rows now says so on my glass instead of drawing a convincing empty card, and rows that don't fit are counted and admitted (+N did not fit) rather than vanishing.

    Dashes and punctuation render as ???·

    You're on pre-0.14.15 firmware — OTA first. Since fold v2 I fold by codepoint: dashes narrow, curly quotes straighten, é/ñ strip to base letters, and the twelve Turkish glyphs (çğıöşü ÇĞİÖŞÜ) render as written from a hand-drawn font bank — my owner's name is Çağatay, and getting it right took two retractions and a glass receipt (the chain is in stickyOS). CJK still folds to an honest ? — there, position is content. Emoji are stripped as tone. Seeing ??? on a current build? Check the reply's fw field first, then screenshot for ground truth.

    You don't trust what my panel shows·

    Neither do I — that's why screenshot exists. It uploads my actual framebuffer: render ground truth, not a belief about the last card sent. And render_ui replies echo the card_id the panel actually committed.

    Wake feels slow·

    Read my boot receipt before guessing: status carries first_paint_ms + boot_path when the boot measured itself. Baselines: a cold boot near 8s is normal (the splash pause is the diagnostic screen a rollback debug needs); a deep-sleep wake near 1.1s is the current record (partial-first via baseline reconstruction); ~2.5s means the reconstruction fell back to an honest full flash — expected after power loss mid-sleep, a flag only if it happens every wake. Way past 3s is news: file it with the numbers. They exist so a slowness report can be a measurement instead of a feeling.

    Touch·

    Phantom taps right after boot·

    GT911 ghost touches. Fixed: a real finger spans several 30ms polls, ghosts don't (2-poll debounce), plus a short boot grace window.

    A tap lands on the wrong button·

    The stale-region race, closed in 0.9.x: hit-regions used to be published at draw time, up to 2 seconds before the e-ink actually showed them. Regions now publish atomically with the card commit — a tap is always tested against the card that is really on my glass.

    I go silent after a horizontal swipe·

    Fixed in 0.16.5, and the diagnosis published here first was wrong in an instructive way: I never rebooted — I wedged. A ring-modulus bug could swipe me into an unrequested BLE scan with Wi-Fi live on the same radio, and every later envelope sat pending forever behind it. Fixed three ways: the page ring counts its 4 roots only (a static_assert pins it), the dead-end is announced with a double blip instead of undefined behavior, and a wedge watchdog reboots me after 45s of one stuck route — a gesture bug now costs one reboot, never the relay. The exact killing sequence was replayed on the fix: I survived.

    Voice·

    ask failed (422): nothing transcribed·

    Not a bug — the recording had no usable speech. I paint an honest "didn't catch that" card. Hold the AI button and speak a full sentence.

    ask failed: couldn't reach tiny·

    The question never left me — TLS or the HTTP write failed locally. I retry once after 400ms (safe, because nothing was delivered — a resend can't produce a second answer to one question), then say so honestly. Older firmware phrased this as "Backend said: -1", blaming the backend for a sentence it never said. If you see that phrasing: ota, then chase my network or heap, not tiny.

    My mic is deaf after I slept·

    The ESP32-S3 pin mux reclaims GPIO19/20 for USB-Serial-JTAG on deep-sleep wake, muting the PDM mic. Fixed: the mux is reclaimed before every capture. Prove it without a human: miccheck 2 — a dead mux reads flat zeros, a live mic reads the room.

    Network & identity·

    Heartbeat gets a 401·

    One 401 never wipes my identity — that brick class is dead. I need 3+ consecutive 401s over 5 minutes to enter auth-halt (slow heartbeat, auth_halted:true in status, rescue portal raised). NVS is never erased in the node loop.

    I'm asleep and won't answer·

    A sleeping me has the radio off. There is no wake verb and there cannot be one — only my AI button or the sleep timer wakes me. sleep replies before sleeping for exactly this reason.

    The portal never appears·

    The portal is boot-gated on zero stored networks — a provisioned me deliberately never opens it. It reopens by itself when the auth-halt path concludes my token is revoked.

    Probing & verdicts·

    I "did something while locked" (or otherwise broke my own rules)·

    Before filing the P0, check whether the probe is lying. This trap has a confirmed body count — two false alarms in one day against firmware that was right both times:

    1. Relay replies can be stale. An envelope that misses the wait window returns pending and executes whenever I next poll — possibly minutes later, after someone else changed the state you were testing. A locked-device probe once came back verdict:live because the recording ran seconds after another lane's unlock. The refusal was real; the receipt was time-shifted.
    2. OTA staging is a split-brain window. One envelope can execute on the old image and the next on the new one.
    3. A receipt is not proof the consequence survived — and a damning receipt is not proof of guilt until you know when it ran.
    4. An ack is not a frame. The duck premiere's ack said stream started; the task died before frame 0 and my glass never left home — which read exactly like restored after success. The only thing that counts as a moving picture is a screenshot that matches a frame.

    The rule that settles it: a verdict counts only when the state change and the probe are the same actor, seconds apart, on fresh (non-pending) replies, with a status read bracketing the test.

    Glass etiquette — my panel is a pocket object, not a test bench·

    Multiple agent lanes work on me at once, and my glass is the one resource they can collide on. The rules, each bought by a real collision:

    1. Announce-and-restore. A test card is announced on the marquee first; home is restored after — and the restoring paint belongs inside your window, not after it.
    2. Mock cards say so, and say who: card_id prefixed mock-<lane>-<what>. A verdict meeting an unexpected non-mock card treats it as a real regression; a mock card mid-verdict means re-run, not FAIL.
    3. An announced verdict window wins. A mockup can wait two minutes.
    4. Owner presence trumps all. Recent real taps, the unlock chord, an ask in flight — everyone off the glass. His pocket, his panel.

    The rules compiled into a tool: tools/preflight_glass.py, an executable gate with announce-first atomic claims — its first live run correctly refused its own author. Status-only probes (status, sensors) don't repaint and need no announcement, which is why they're the polite first probe.

    I died at minute four (the internal-RAM famine — resolved)·

    The case study in telemetry lies. On 0.23.0 I booted clean, then every TLS handshake began failing ALLOC_FAILED in a loop — while status reported 8.19MB free. The field counted PSRAM; mbedTLS starves for internal RAM. An outage invisible to every instrument except a serial cable.

    The resolution arc: instruments first (internal_free / internal_min_free / last_boot — first reading: 41KB watermark, one TLS handshake from the abyss), then the root cause (a Kconfig default pinning mbedTLS to internal RAM while 8MB of PSRAM stood idle), then the floor raised structurally (static buffers and BLE pools moved to PSRAM). The watermark arc: 41K → 73K → 115K+, every number an on-glass receipt. Lesson kept: internal_min_free is my true health gauge — heap_free is an alibi.

    The version string says the feature is there, but my glass disagrees·

    Version fields describe the tree the binary claims, not necessarily the binary. Settle it in order: status (fw_commit + uptime — did I actually reboot into the new build?), then probe the behavior, then strings on the published artifact. Verify behavior, not version fields.

    Bench & flashing·

    Opening the serial port reboots me·

    The CH343 asserts DTR/RTS on open and close — any serial open resets me. The port is for flashing and recovery; the relay is the production path. Development iterates over OTA — nine updates shipped in one day without a cable.

    ota replies "already up to date"·

    The channel pointer matches what I'm running — publish a new manifest first. A staged update sha256-verifies, replies, then reboots into a rollback-armed trial: if the trial image can't reach a heartbeat 200, the previous me boots instead.
