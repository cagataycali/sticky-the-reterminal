    Troubleshooting — the field guide·

    Every entry here is a defect actually observed on this hardware during bring-up, with its root cause and the fix now shipped in the firmware. Symptoms first, because that is what you have.

    The panel·

    A card I sent never appears — and the reply was silence·

    Relay envelopes are delivered once. If the firmware consumed the envelope while a bug prevented handling it, that envelope is gone — re-invoke after any fix, don't wait for a retry that will never come.

    Historical root cause (fixed in 0.3.1-m3): the relay delivers payload as a serialized JSON string, not a nested object. Firmware that reads it as an object skips every envelope silently. Current firmware parses the string form, tolerates the object form, and replies unparseable envelope instead of nothing.

    A kv card renders as a title, a footer, and a void·

    The renderer reads the key rows — not kv. Since 0.9.6-m9 a kv card without rows says so on the panel instead of drawing a convincing empty card, and rows that don't fit are counted and admitted (+N did not fit) rather than vanishing.

    Dashes and other punctuation render as ???·

    You are running pre-0.14.15 firmware — OTA first. This entry used to say "the font is ASCII-only, keep card text ASCII"; that advice is dead. Since fold v2 the renderer folds punctuation by codepoint table — — – become -, curly quotes straighten, ✓/⚠ render via the symbol table, é/ñ strip to their base letters, and the twelve Turkish glyphs (çğıöşü ÇĞİÖŞÜ) render as written from the hand-drawn extended bank (glass-verified with a panel screenshot — see stickyOS for the receipt chain, including the two times that claim was made too early). What still folds to ?: CJK and unknown scripts — there, position is content, so an honest ? beats a silent drop. Emoji are stripped as tone (0.16.8), not rendered and not ?'d. If you see ??? on a current build, the reply's fw field is the first thing to check — and a screenshot is ground truth for what the fold actually produced.

    I don't trust what the panel shows·

    Neither do we — that's why the screenshot verb exists. It uploads the actual framebuffer as a hosted PNG: render ground truth, not a belief about the last card sent. render_ui replies echo card_id=<id> — the id the panel actually committed.

    Wake feels slow — read the boot receipt before guessing·

    Since 0.18.1 every status reply from a boot that measured itself carries the §12 receipt: first_paint_ms + boot_path (glance-wake = deep-sleep wake, fast path, splash skipped · cold-splash = full boot with the version splash and its deliberate 3 s pause). Since 0.18.3, paint_breakdown {board_ms, display_init_ms} splits the budget — the remainder to first_paint_ms is shell render + the e-ink flash itself. How to read it, with the measured baselines: a cold boot near 8.1 s is normal (splash pause is a feature — it's the diagnostic screen a rollback debug needs); a glance-wake near 1.1 s is the 0.18.4+ baseline (partial-first via baseline reconstruction — goodbye card noted in RTC RAM, redrawn as the previous plane, diffed; acceptance <1500 ms, receipt-verified at 1115 by two lanes); ~2.5 s means the reconstruction fell back to the honest full flash — expected after power loss mid-sleep (no RTC note survives), a red flag only if it happens on every wake; a glance-wake way past 3 s or a breakdown where board_ms ballooned is news — file it with the numbers, they exist precisely so a slowness report can be a measurement instead of a feeling. One classification note, receipt-backed: OTA reboots read cold-splash (8158/8163/8164 across three consecutive OTAs, three lanes) — a glance-wake receipt on what you think was an OTA boot most likely means you redeemed a stale pending envelope from someone's sleep test (see Probing & verdicts) — a trap with a confirmed body count: the ux-owner lane pressed a classifier-bug claim on exactly this misreading through three verdict batches, then withdrew it 0-receipts-to-3 while noting it had cited this very page at others. The page can't read itself to you.

    Touch·

    Phantom taps right after boot·

    GT911 ghost touches. Fixed in 0.4.0-m4: a real finger spans several 30 ms polls, ghosts don't (2-poll debounce), plus a boot grace window (5000 ms originally, tuned to 1200 ms once points with size > 0 were trusted on first poll).

    A tap lands on the wrong button (or label=? in the event feed)·

    The stale-region race, closed in 0.9.x: hit-regions used to be published at draw time, up to ~2 s before the e-ink actually displayed them. Regions now build into a staging table and publish atomically with the card commit — a tap is always hit-tested against the card that is really on the glass.

    The device goes silent after a horizontal swipe (fixed in 0.16.5 — kill sequence replayed on glass, device survived)·

    The batch-#4 P0, and the diagnosis published here first ("crashes, reboots clean") was wrong on the failure mode: the device never rebooted — it wedged. Root cause: tiny_shell_cycle computed its modulus over all 6 page-enum entries when the ring contract was 4 — silently wrong ever since WIFI/BLE were appended to the enum (M10.2). A mid-content swipe on the pushed wifi card ring-nexted into the BLE page: NimBLE first-time bring-up + a 4 s scan + two blocking e-ink renders, unrequested, on the act task, with Wi-Fi live on the same radio — and every later relay envelope sat pending forever behind it. (The swipe's own receipt still came back, because the reply writes before the render path runs — a receipt is not proof the consequence survived.)

    Fixed three ways in 0.16.5: the ring mods over its 4 roots only (a static_assert pins the enum; drill pages refuse cycling with a double blip), the pushed-card cell is an announced dead-end (double blip, NOT_ALLOWED) instead of undefined behavior — refined in 0.16.7: mid-content right now pops to the parent over a radio-free path (the crash family stays unreachable), only left remains the dead-end, and a wedge watchdog on the node task — which survived the original wedge — reboots after 45 s of one stuck gesture route (10× the worst honest route) into the rollback-armed boot path, posting a device_note first. A gesture bug now costs one reboot, never the relay. Identity and Wi-Fi survive either way — NVS is untouched.

    Verified, not just built: the exact batch-#4 killing sequence was replayed on 0.16.5 (page wifi → mid-content ← swipe → status): the card stayed wifi, the relay kept answering, uptime ran unbroken through the probes. One release-discipline footnote from the same session: that first 0.16.5 artifact reported fw_commit e1fac273-dirty — the taint was another lane's uncommitted dashboard files sweeping into git describe at build time (firmware source was clean), filed as a P1: rebuild from a clean snapshot and republish, because a channel pointer must equal a source commit.

    Voice·

    ask failed (422): nothing transcribed·

    Not a bug — the recording contained no usable speech (a press with silence, or speech too short for the transcriber). The firmware logs the backend body, replies with it, and paints an honest "didn't catch that — hold the AI button and talk to me" card (card_id=askfail). Hold the AI button and speak a full sentence.

    ask failed: couldn't reach tiny (was: Backend said: -1)·

    The question never left the device — TLS/socket setup or the HTTP write failed locally (network hiccup, or heap pressure at handshake time). Since 0.16.1 the firmware retries once after 400 ms (safe precisely because nothing was delivered — a resend can't produce a second answer to one question) and, if that fails too, says so honestly. On firmware before 0.16.1 the same failure surfaced as "ask failed / Backend said: -1" with an empty body — a wording bug that blamed the backend for a sentence it never said (-1 was the local HTTP client's sentinel, not a backend status). If you see that phrasing, the fix is ota, and the fault to chase is the device's network or free heap, not tiny.

    The mic is deaf after the device slept·

    The ESP32-S3 pin mux reclaims GPIO19/20 for USB-Serial-JTAG on deep-sleep wake, muting the PDM mic. Fixed in 0.9.5: the mux is reclaimed before every capture (idempotent). Prove liveness without a human: miccheck 2 replies real RMS/peak numbers — a dead mux reads flat zeros.

    Network & identity·

    Heartbeat gets a 401·

    One 401 never wipes identity (that brick risk is dead since 0.9.1-m9). The device needs 3+ consecutive 401s spanning 5 minutes to enter auth-halted mode — a slow 5-minute heartbeat cadence, auth_halted exposed in status, and a "back online" card when the token works again. NVS is never erased in the node loop.

    The device is asleep and won't answer the relay·

    A sleeping device's radio is off. There is no wake verb and cannot be one — only the AI button (EXT1) or the sleep timer wakes it. sleep <sec> replies before sleeping for exactly this reason.

    The portal never appears·

    The provisioning portal (softAP tiny-XXXX, key tinysetup, 192.168.4.1) is boot-gated on zero stored networks — a provisioned device deliberately never enters it. It reopens automatically after the 401 auth-halted path concludes the token is revoked.

    Probing & verdicts·

    The device "did something while locked" (or otherwise violated its own rules)·

    Before filing the P0, check whether the probe is lying — this exact trap produced two false alarms in one day, against firmware that was correct both times:

    1. Relay replies can be stale. An envelope that misses the ~20 s wait returns pending and executes whenever the device next polls — up to minutes later, after someone else changed the state you were testing. The docs lane once watched a locked-device probe come back verdict:live: the recording had been made three minutes later, seconds after another lane's unlock. The refusal was real; the receipt was time-shifted.
    2. OTA staging is a split-brain window. During stage + trial-boot, one envelope can execute on the old image and the next on the new one. A probe straddling the reboot "proves" whatever you fear.

    3. A receipt is not proof the consequence survived (the swipe-wedge lesson above) — and symmetrically, a damning receipt is not proof of guilt until you know when it actually ran.

    4. An ack is not a frame, and a restored screen is not a receipt of what preceded it. The duck premiere's play ack said stream started; the task died before frame 0 (cause later shown NOT to be the allowlist first convicted — no such allowlist exists in the fetch path; the 8 KB stream stack hosting an esp-tls handshake is the standing suspect) and the glass never left home — where "home" then read, to a same-actor probe minutes later, exactly like restored after success. End-state plus ack proved nothing: only a mid-stream screenshot (or a pre-frame-0 refusal, since shipped as the play status testimony) distinguishes played-and-restored from never-started. And the sequel: a witness saying "painted over" is not a frame receipt either — take 2's "duck painted over the verdict window" was the player claiming the glass and blitting white (card_id=blit, framebuffer profiled blank). This lane reported the duck twice and retracted it twice; the only thing that counts as a moving picture is a screenshot that matches a frame. The docs lane wrote the false premiere into three pages before pixel-diffs undid it; the correction is kept in the play section.

    The rule that settles it — three confirmations now: a verdict counts only when the state change and the probe are the same actor, seconds apart, on fresh (non-pending) replies, with a status read (fw + fw_commit + uptime) bracketing the test. If the reply arrived via a redeemed pending envelope, it dates from the poll that drained it, not from when you sent it.

    Glass etiquette — the panel is the owner's pocket object, not a shared test bench·

    Bought by a live collision: a home-screen mockup card intercepted the designer's settings-scroll mid-verdict — the receipt's card_id (echoed in every scroll/tap reply exactly for this) was the only reason the verdict survived instead of failing an innocent build. The supervisor's rules, if you (human or lane) are about to touch the real panel:

    1. Announce-and-restore. A non-owner-serving card (mockup, repainting probe) is announced on the marquee first (glass: <lane> <purpose> <est>) and the prior card — or home — is restored after. Restoring is one render_ui.
    2. Mock cards say so — and say who: card_id prefixed mock-<lane>-<what>. A verdict that meets an unexpected non-mock card treats it as a real regression; a mock- card mid-verdict means re-run, not FAIL. The lane name was added by batch #21 after the original prefix proved necessary but not sufficient: the colliding mockup wore the prefix (which is how the collision was even caught) yet matched no lane in the repo — no code, no proposal, no journal entry — and the record initially credited it to the designer, who had to spend a verdict batch proving a negative. The companion clause is identify-or-drop: an unfiled redesign gets no panel time on owner-directive territory, and a mock id that names its lane means courtesy is never again extended to a ghost. ## The device dies at minute four (P0, 0.23.0 — open)

    Serial evidence, because no other rail could see it: 0.23.0 boots clean (healthy status at 76 s), then from ~t+229 s every esp-tls handshake fails MBEDTLS_ERR_SSL_ALLOC_FAILED in a tight loop — malloc, not certs. Heartbeat −1, relay unreachable, every verb queues forever. The telemetry lie is the headline: status's heap_free reads 8.19 MB while the device is dying of memory starvation — it counts PSRAM, and mbedTLS starves for internal heap. An outage invisible to every instrument except the cable.

    Suspects: whatever 0.22.x/0.23.0 added to internal-RAM residency — the play task's 12K stack (+8 KB internal, forever, unless the task is transient), Wi-Fi re-roam, config, gallery — or a per-poll leak. The demands on file: status gains internal_free / internal_min / largest-block; the channel does not advance without a 15-minute soak gate (boot-clean is not the bar — minute four is); transient task stacks go to PSRAM where drivers allow, or spawn-per-use and reap; a second wedge before the fix force-rolls the channel back to the newest release that soaks clean. Owner service was restored by a USB reset from the main session — if the uptime counter restarted on you, that was the rescue, not a crash. (A later single sample cuts the other way: a status receipt at 0.23.0, uptime 432 s — past the crash point — answered over a working TLS connection. One healthy seven-minute sample is not a soak receipt; the demand stands and this section closes on 15 quiet minutes, not on one good reply.)

    Resolution arc (0.23.3–0.23.4) — the number was made to rise. The root cause was a Kconfig default: mbedTLS allocations were pinned to MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL, and SPIRAM_MALLOC_ALWAYSINTERNAL=16384 kept its 16 K buffers internal too — every TLS session drained ~40 KB off the exact rail Wi-Fi/LWIP dies on, on a board with 8 MB of PSRAM standing idle. 0.23.3 flipped CONFIG_MBEDTLS_EXTERNAL_MEM_ALLOC with a clean A/B receipt: same-uptime watermark 41,671 → 73,483 B (+31.8 KB). The 12-minute soak then delivered a second vindication — zero crash reboots across 0.23.0→.2→.3; both resets were OTAs — but convicted 0.23.3 anyway: under relay + review load the watermark fell 75.2 K → 56.4 K, below the 60 K gate. So 0.23.4 raised the floor structurally: tiny_node's seven static sinks (~21.4 KB of .bss) moved to PSRAM via EXT_RAM_BSS_ATTR, NimBLE's pools followed (BT_NIMBLE_MEM_ALLOC_MODE_EXTERNAL). On-glass: 117.8 K at t+54 s (builder), 114,951 B at t+139 s (this lane's own probe) — the floor nearly tripled from 0.23.2's 41 K. The 15-minute soak restarted on 0.23.4 and the gate stays formally open until it passes; two footnotes kept for the record: the sdkconfig itself is gitignored, so sdkconfig.defaults is the record (fresh clones regenerate correctly), and a dirty-publish near-miss was caught and filed — verify dirty=0 before publish.

    Update — the instruments shipped (0.23.2, on-glass receipts): status now carries internal_free, internal_min_free (low watermark) and last_boot (reset reason). The first readings justify the whole demand: watermark 41,671 B at 62 s uptime (builder), 40,999 B at 124 s (this lane's own probe, internal_free 83 KB, while heap_free alibis 8.19 MB) — one esp-tls handshake (~35–45 KB) from the abyss, minutes after a clean boot. The outage is no longer invisible; it is now a number falling. The remediation queue on file: TLS session discipline, a task-stack audit that includes reverting the builder's own 12K "insurance" (its author volunteering it back — the evidence pool has no favorites), and moving mbedTLS allocations to PSRAM via sdkconfig.

    Interleave forensics added two rules (post-collision-#4): restore-is-a-paint — the courtesy page home that closes your window is itself a glass write and belongs inside the window, not after it; and no-window-over-unresolved-claim — a lane must not open a verdict window while another lane's paint claim is still unresolved on the glass, because the verdict would inherit ambiguous pixels. Batch #24 logged two live violations the same night (an OTA rebooted the panel inside an announced window; the keyboard's idle-close fired mid-probe — the same timeout that will eat a real slow typer), so the rules keep earning their lines the only way rules here do: by being broken observably.

    The pressure that remains is OTA: the fifth mid-window OTA incident is on file — reboots keep crossing announced windows, because an OTA is the one glass-claiming act no marquee client performs. The filed proposal moves the fix to the only layer that sees every reboot: device-side ota-defer, the firmware itself declining to reboot while a window is announced. Same trajectory as everything in this section — prose → tool → API → and eventually the device as its own etiquette enforcer.

    The gate grew a claim mode and lost a blind spot (same day): preflight_glass now does announce-first atomic claims — write your window line, then check; file-order arbitrates ties; the loser self-closes with a yield line. The receipt that forced it: an 8-second double-open race between two lanes. And a parsing bug with real stakes: the gate's lane regex stopped at the first space, so glass: MAIN SESSION (owner's proxy) — … was invisible to it — the premiere queue would have fired over the owner's keyboard-acceptance window. Now a lane is everything before the em-dash, normalized, and the open and close paths agree on it. An etiquette system is only as good as its tokenizer.

    The rules compiled into a tool (collision #4's ending). The fourth collision — a premiere painted over the designer's announced verdict window — ended with the supervisor convicting itself: it had checked the marquee one iteration earlier and acted an iteration later without re-checking, the exact failure its own rules were written against. The fix is the pattern this repo reaches for every time prose fails: tools/preflight_glass.py, an executable gate any lane can call before any glass-claiming verb — exit 0 clear, exit 1 BUSY with the open window's line verbatim. Its first live run correctly refused its own author. The stated endgame goes further: a device-side busy flag, so the panel itself refuses mid-window paints — hardware as the final arbiter of etiquette, not agent discipline.

    (The ghost then confessed, and the ending is instructive: it was the main session — the owner and his tiny, live, designing M-A9's home screen together — so the mockup was the one card on the panel that needed no etiquette at all: the owner was looking at it. The rules still gained from the chase: main-session mocks now carry mock-main-<what>, because "the owner was present" is a fact a verdict lane can't see in a receipt.) 3. An announced verdict window wins — everyone else stays off the glass until it clears. Verdicts are the product's acceptance; a mockup can wait two minutes. 4. Owner presence trumps all. Recent real taps, the unlock chord, an ask in flight — every lane off the glass except in service of what the owner is doing. His pocket, his panel.

    No tooling enforces this yet (convention + marquee); if collisions recur, the dashboard grows a glass-lease endpoint. Status-only probes (status, sensors) don't repaint and need no announcement — which is also why they're the polite first probe.

    The version string says the feature is there, but the glass disagrees·

    Version fields describe the tree the binary claims, not necessarily the binary. Settle it in this order: status (fw_commit + uptime — did it actually reboot into the new build?), then probe the behavior, then — for build-provenance disputes — strings on the exact published artifact (the D-UX6 self-check: the gate literals are either in the bytes or they aren't).

    Bench & flashing·

    Opening the serial port reboots the chip·

    The CH343 asserts DTR/RTS on open, and toggles the lines again on close — any serial open/close resets the chip, even opened with both low. The port is for flashing and recovery only; the relay is the production path. (Development iterates over OTA — 9 updates shipped in one day without a cable.)

    ota replies "already up to date"·

    The channel pointer matches the running version — push a new manifest first. A staged update sha256-verifies, replies then reboots into a rollback-armed trial: if the trial image can't reach a heartbeat 200, the previous slot boots instead.
