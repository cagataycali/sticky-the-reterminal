    THINKING.md — a deep-dive session on what stickyOS actually is·

    2026-08-25, late evening. Written by the main-session tiny after one bring-up day: 12 OTAs, a keyboard on glass, an iOS remote in cagatay's pocket, two loops still running. This is the thinking the commit log doesn't hold — read it before making the next big call.

    1. What we actually built today (get the category right)·

    The Sticky is not a display peripheral and not an IoT gadget. It's the fifth embodiment of one agent identity (phone, web, camera-necklace, voice-necklace, and now a wall). The category test from the MHS essay applies verbatim: a robot is anything that can introduce itself — readable state, changeable state with declared limits, callable procedures, announcements. The Sticky passed that test by ~18:00 today: status (readable), page/render_ui (changeable, bounded), verbs advertised in a capability list (declared), device_note events (announcements).

    What makes it different from its four siblings: it's the first embodiment that is a PLACE, not a companion. The phone travels, the necklace is worn, the web follows the login. The Sticky is bolted to a wall in one room of one home. Every design decision downstream should flow from that: it serves whoever stands in front of it, its content should assume the household, and its latency contract is glanceable, not interactive-first.

    2. The physics is the design language (stop fighting the panel)·

    Every failed UI idea today failed the same way: pretending the panel is an LCD. The working ideas all scheduled around the panel. Write the latency budget on the wall:

    Event Budget Mechanism
    Tap acknowledged < 50ms buzzer (audio is the fast lane)
    Tap visually confirmed 300–500ms partial refresh, region-sized
    Page change 1–2s full refresh (the metronome)
    Relay verb round-trip 5–15s poll cadence + agent turn
    Ask with tools 10–60s backend agent loop

    The deep principle: on e-ink, UI is a series of commitments, not a stream of frames. A phone UI animates toward a state; the Sticky declares states. That's why the notification model matters so much (§4): interruptions are expensive when every repaint costs 1–2s of visible flicker, so the OS must own when the glass changes, and everything else queues. Same reason double-tap and drag were rejected in the touch audit — they're promises the panel can't keep. Design verbs the panel CAN keep: tap, swipe-as-command (not swipe-as-scroll-physics), long-press, and time (cards that age gracefully).

    Corollary nobody should forget: audio is the low-latency channel. The buzzer is the only sub-100ms output this device has. Chimes, clicks, and error tones aren't polish — they're the half of the interaction loop the panel physically cannot provide.

    3. "Phone replacement" — deconstructed honestly·

    Cagatay's framing ("mobile phone replacement", "stickyOS to go") is directionally right and literally wrong, and the difference is the product:

    A phone is four things: always-with-you, an input surface, a notification funnel, and an identity token. The Sticky can be three of them at home — it will never be always-with-you (that's the necklace's job). So the honest thesis:

    The Sticky replaces the phone in the room it lives in. Reaching for your phone to ask a question, check a status, send a message while standing in your kitchen — that's the behavior it deletes. The phone becomes the mobile terminal; the Sticky is the domestic one.

    This resolves the tension in the directives: iOS design parity (§6 of phase 3) isn't about making the Sticky be an iPhone — it's about making it recognizably the same tiny so switching rooms doesn't mean switching mental models. Same pages, same names, same hierarchy; different physics.

    It also predicts the endgame (§7): one Sticky is a domestic terminal; N stickies are a house-scale UI. Kitchen shows the morning brief, door shows weather + calendar on the way out, desk shows the build status. The fleet already has the primitives (per-device relay, cards, presence). "stickyOS to go" ends with the home being the device.

    4. Architecture tensions that will bite (ranked, with positions taken)·

    T1 — Poll vs push. The 5s relay poll is both the latency floor (§2 table) and the battery ceiling. 750mAh with always-on Wi-Fi + 5s HTTPS polls is a days-not-weeks device; the audit's adaptive-poll directive helps but doesn't change the shape. Position: keep polling for v1 (it's simple, NAT-proof, and proven today), but the M-next experiment is long-poll: hold the GET open 30–60s server-side, reply on envelope arrival. Same firmware surface (esp_http_client), near-instant push, and fewer TLS handshakes than 5s polling. Wake-on-lift (IMU) then gates the whole radio: asleep = 60s cadence, lifted/touched = long-poll. That combination is the difference between "charge it weekly" and "why is it dead again".

    T2 — Who owns the glass. Today a remote render_ui stomps whatever page the user is on — already directed to fix via the notification model, but state the deeper rule: stickyOS needs a focus concept. The local shell is the compositor; remote content is a request for focus, not a seizure of it. Home screen = remote cards land immediately (it's ambient). Any other page = dot + notifications queue. The one exception: safety/alarm class cards (a priority field the card spec doesn't have yet — add it before someone builds an alert feature on a queue that can silently defer it).

    T3 — The verb grammar is now a 3-consumer contract (dashboard, iOS panel, relay/agent). It's held together by string prefixes and shared discipline. Before phase 3 widens it (messages, images, scroll state), pin it: a grammar_version in the status reply + the capability list already advertised. The iOS panel and dashboard check it and degrade politely. Cheap now, impossible to retrofit after a fleet of stickies exists.

    T4 — Keystroke privacy set a precedent — name it. The keyboard's k: taps never leave the device. Generalize: local-only zones — input surfaces whose events are consumed by the shell and never posted upstream. The Wi-Fi password keyboard is one; a future PIN pad, a "private note" composer. Make it a card attribute ("local": true), not a keyboard special case. This is the kind of promise that, kept structurally, becomes a reason to trust the whole fleet.

    T5 — The agent is the OS vendor, and it ships 12 times a day. Today's cadence (build → OTA → verify-on-glass → journal) worked because the rollback judge (first heartbeat 200 marks valid) and sha pinning were built in M6, before velocity. What's missing at this speed: a canary ritual for the day there are two stickies (update one, watch a full heartbeat+verb cycle, then the rest) and a factory-image discipline (one known-good build burned to the recovery partition quarterly). Velocity is only a virtue while the device can always crawl home. Related honesty: today's "version-string lie" (0.7.0 reporting before m7a code shipped) is the failure mode of agent-speed shipping — the fix (behavior probes over version fields) belongs in the supervisor's permanent checklist, not just today's memory.

    T6 — The provisioning gap is the shippability gap. Everything else on the roadmap is polish; softAP provisioning is the difference between "cagatay's device" and "a device". It's correctly next in the builder queue. Hold the line: no new feature work should jump it (keyboard already exists — the portal can reuse it, which is the elegant closure: the first thing the on-glass keyboard provisions is the device itself).

    5. The most interesting thing here (the part worth a blog post)·

    stickyOS is being developed almost entirely by agent loops — builder writes firmware, supervisor verifies on glass, main session translates human intent into directives, and the product itself is a surface for the same agent. The loop closes fully when the Sticky's own ask verb reaches the full toolset (phase-3 #1): at that point the device can be asked to describe, diagnose, and file issues against its own firmware — the machine introduces itself not just to the network but to its own development process.

    That's the same recursion as the necklace essay (an agent designs a body, a printer makes it, the object is where the next agent lives) — but sharper: the Sticky is the first tiny embodiment whose operating system is a nightly artifact of the agent that inhabits it. Bench discipline is what keeps that from being scary instead of delightful: every claim journaled with evidence, every OTA reversible, every "it works" backed by a screenshot of the glass or a human finger. Today produced multiple instances of the pattern "the machine said done; the glass disagreed; the glass won." Keep the glass winning.

    6. Ranked risks (what actually threatens the mission)·

    1. Orientation surgery (M10.O) — touches the renderer's deepest invariant (canvas/touch/screenshot agreement, verified in audit). Sequenced correctly (after Wi-Fi keyboard). Demand the arrow-up test card in all 4 orientations × touch hit-test before it merges.
    2. Battery honesty — no measurement exists yet of real draw. Before adaptive polling is designed, spend one loop iteration measuring: current at 5s poll idle, at long-poll idle, asleep. Design against numbers, not vibes (the bench rule: an artifact declares what it IS).
    3. Font/UTF-8 debt — every text surface built before the real font lands inherits ASCII-fold shame ("Cagatay" on the glass of a Turkish speaker's kitchen). It's scheduled; don't let it slip behind feature work again.
    4. Single-unit fleet — every OTA bets the only device. Mitigated by rollback + recovery doc; properly fixed by the second unit (canary, §T5).
    5. Context debt in the loops — builder loop v1 died of a 15-min inline wait; v2 learned reply-then-act. The lesson generalizes: the loops' own working discipline is part of the architecture now and belongs in LOOP_BRIEF.md, not tribal memory.

    7. Where this goes (12-month shape, so near-term calls aim somewhere)·

    • 1 Sticky (now): domestic terminal, stickyOS phases 2–3, battery truth.
    • 2–3 Stickies: canary OTA ritual; rooms get roles; presence-aware cards (the kitchen one knows it's morning); the notification model becomes a house-wide router ("show this where someone is standing").
    • Sticky as product: softAP portal + factory image + the enrollment story make it giftable. The e-ink tile becomes the calm face of tiny — the anti-doomscroll surface: no feed, no infinite anything, cards that age.
    • The MHS rhyme: a Sticky declares bounds (refresh budget, local-only zones, battery gates) in its capability surface. It's the same thesis at domestic scale: safety and honesty as properties of the device, not paragraphs in a prompt. When the embargo lifts and the essay ships, the Sticky is quietly its best demo: any machine can introduce itself — even a fridge magnet.

    Filed by the main session. Supervisor v2: hold T2 (focus/priority field), T3 (grammar_version) and risk #2 (battery measurement) against the phase-3 queue — they're cheap now and expensive later. Everything else is direction, not work items.
